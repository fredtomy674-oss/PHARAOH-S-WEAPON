■ Note that
9 < 11 < 16
is an inequality of three sides which means that:
The number 11 is greater than the number 9 (11 > 9)
and the number 11 is less than the number 16 (11 < 16)
which means that: The number 11 lies between the two numbers 9 and 16.

Can a number be both rational and irrational at the same time?

✔
▶ Each of the following numbers is a rational number
-12, √25, 3√-27, 2 2/5, 0.125, 3.6
because each can be written in the form of a/b where a and b are integers and b ≠ 0, as follows:
-12 = -12/1
√25 = 5 = 5/1
3√-27 = -3 = -3/1
2 2/5 = 12/5
0.125 = 125/1000 = 1/8
3.6 = 11/3

■ Remember that
The number 3.6 is an infinite decimal (recurring) and it is a rational number where 3.6 = 3.6666... = 11/3

▶ Both numbers √13, -3√10 are irrational numbers.
Because: the number 13 is not a perfect square, the number 10 is not a perfect cube, and therefore neither can be written in the form of a/b where a and b are integers and b ≠ 0.

Finding an approximate value for an irrational number
Any irrational number lies between two rational numbers and it can be represented on the number line by a point that lies between these two numbers.
