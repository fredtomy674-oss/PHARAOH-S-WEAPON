Pythagoras' Theorem
Pythagoras' Theorem is an equation that relates the side lengths of a right-angled triangle. It is named after the Greek mathematician Pythagoras, who lived in the 6th century BC

Pythagoras' Theorem
The area of the square drawn on the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle is equal to the sum of the areas of the squares drawn on the other two sides.

Another form of Pythagoras' theorem:
In a right-angled triangle, the square of the hypotenuse length is equal to the sum of the squares of the other two side lengths.

Thus: c² = a² + b² where a and b are the lengths of the sides of the right angle of the triangle, and c is the length of the hypotenuse.
