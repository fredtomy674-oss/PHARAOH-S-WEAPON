Cases of Congruent Triangles

First Case: Side - Side - Side (SSS)
Two triangles are congruent if each side of one triangle is congruent to
its corresponding side of the other triangle.
If AB ≅ DE, BC ≅ EF, AC ≅ DF
then Δ ABC ≅ Δ DEF

Example 2
In the given figure:
The point D is the midpoint of BC, AB = AC, m (∠ B) = 63°
1 Prove that: Δ ADB ≅ Δ ADC
2 Find by proof: m (∠ DAC)

∵ The point D is the midpoint of BC
∴ BD = DC
∵ In Δ ADB and Δ ADC:
AB ≅ AC (given) BD ≅ CD (proved) AD ≅ AD (common side)
∴ Δ ADB ≅ Δ ADC (required 1)
From the congruence:
m (∠ C) = m (∠ B) = 63° , m (∠ DAC) = m (∠ DAB)
In Δ ABC:
∴ m (∠ CAB) = 180° - (63° + 63°) = 54°
∴ m (∠ DAC) = m (∠ DAB) = 54° / 2 = 27° (required 2)

Second Case: Side - Angle - Side (SAS)
Two triangles are congruent if two sides and the included angle of
one triangle are congruent to their corresponding elements of the
other triangle.
If AB ≅ DE, ∠ B ≅ ∠ E, BC ≅ EF
then Δ ABC ≅ Δ DEF
