Inference
This is when you figure out
something new by combining
clues from the text with what
you already know. The author
doesn't say it directly, but
gives you hints.
Example: Rokaya describes
Tokyo as "organized" and
"people are" "polite." Infer:
She appreciates order and good
manners. (She doesn't state
this directly, but her words
show this meaning.)

Reading Tips
• Be a Reading
Detective!
• When you read,
think like a detective
to uncover hidden
meanings and identify
what's real.
Facts: These are clues
that are directly stated
in the text. You can
point right to the
words.
Example: "Rokaya
visited Japan." (The
text tells you this.)
Opinions: These
are what someone
thinks or feels. They
often use words that
show judgment or
preference.
Example: "Tokyo is one
of the most organized
places." (This is
Rokaya's personal
view.)
