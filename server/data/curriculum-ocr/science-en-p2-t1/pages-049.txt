Indications of the occurrence of a chemical Reaction

There are many different indications which can indicate the occurrence of a chemical reaction.
Team up with your classmates - under the supervision of your teacher - to conduct
the following experiments in the laboratory to identify these indications.

1 Formation of a precipitate

Activity 2 Practical

Materials and Tools Used:
• Sodium chloride solution. • Silver nitrate solution.
• Test tube. • Dropper.

Steps :
1 Put a small quantity of sodium chloride solution in
the test tube.
2 Add drops of silver nitrate solution gradually.
What do you observe? ........................................................
What do you conclude? ........................................................

Figure (5)
Reaction accompanied
by precipitate formation

Adding silver nitrate solution AgNO3 to sodium chloride solution NaCl causes the occurrence
of a chemical reaction, indicated by the formation of a white precipitate of silver chloride
AgCl which does not dissolve (insoluble) in water and its colour is changed into violet when
it is exposed to sunlight (Figure 5).

2 Colour change

Activity 3 Practical

Materials and Tools Used:
• A zinc plate. • Copper sulphate solution.
• A beaker.

Steps :
1 Put copper sulphate solution in the beaker.
2 Put the zinc plate in the solution.
What do you observe? ........................................................
What do you conclude? ........................................................

Figure (6)
Reaction accompanied by
a colour change

Placing the zinc plate in the copper sulphate solution causes a chemical reaction, indicated
by the disappearance of the blue colour of the copper sulphate solution CuSO4, due to
the formation of colourless zinc sulphate solution ZnSO4 and reddish brown copper is
deposited over the zinc plate (Figure 6).
