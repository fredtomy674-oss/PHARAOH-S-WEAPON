Chapter 5

With trembling hands, he prepared his harpoon. He knew this

moment would decide everything. The fish surfaced again, circling
near the boat, its dorsal fin cutting the water. Santiago gathered his
strength, lifted the harpoon high, and when the fish came close, he

drove the iron deep into its heart.
The marlin leapt, shining in the air one last time, then crashed into
the sea with a thunderous splash. Its body shook, the water boiled,

and then it was still. The great fish floated beside the boat, slowly

turning on its side, its silver scales catching the sun.
Santiago dropped the harpoon and leaned against the mast, shaking

with exhaustion and triumph. Tears filled his eyes, not only from

victory but from sorrow. “I have killed the most noble of creatures,”
he whispered. “He did not deserve to die, but I needed to live.”
With careful hands, the old man tied the marlin to the side of his
skiff. Its body was so large that it stretched far beyond the boat’s

length. The fish was heavy, majestic, a prize greater than anything

Santiago had ever caught.
For a moment, he allowed himself to feel pride. “They will not
believe me,” he thought. “They will say no man could take such a

fish alone. But I know. And the boy will know. That is enough.”
