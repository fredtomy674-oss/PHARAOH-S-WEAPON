At last, silence returned. No more sharks came. The marlin’s
skeleton glowed white in the moonlight, its long sword rising like a
banner of both triumph and tragedy.

Santiago looked at it and felt tears in his eyes. “You were my
greatest enemy and my greatest friend,” he told the fish. “They

destroyed you, but they could not destroy my pride. We fought
well.”
He thought of the boy, and a faint smile touched his lips. “The boy
will not let me be alone again,” he said. “Tomorrow, he will help me.
He must.”
Santiago steered the little skiff through the dark waters, his body
broken but his spirit still alive. He had lost his catch, but he had not
lost his dignity.
The sea was quiet again, carrying the old man and the skeleton of
the marlin slowly toward the shore.
