10-2 What is Left After Evaporation Reaction between Solutions and Metals Chapter 10

Point! Changes in Metals
! When you evaporate the liquid resulted from aluminum that reacted with dilute hydrochloric acid, a white solid appears.
! Differences from the original metal:
• Appearance: It is not shiny.
• Reaction: Even if you put the remaining solid into dilute hydrochloric acid again, it will not react or produce bubbles like the original metal.
→ We can say that the metal changed into a substance with completely different properties by reacting with the aqueous solution.

Warm Up Let's test what the powder really is!
Let's extract and observe what disappeared when it dissolved in water.
1 Preparation:
• The liquid made in the previous lesson • Beaker • Test tube
• Test tube rack • Pipette • Evaporating dish • Portable gas stove
• Wire gauze • Safety goggles • Dilute hydrochloric acid
2 Experiment:
1 Put the liquid in which the metal reacted into an evaporating dish.
2 Heat it gently on low heat with a portable gas stove.
3 Turn off the heat while there is still a little liquid left, and let the remaining heat dry it.
4 Observe what is left.
5 Try putting the powder you took out into dilute hydrochloric acid in another test tube. Check that no bubbles come out.
[Caution!]
• During the experiment, do not bring your face close to the evaporating dish for breathing in the releasing fumes directly.
• To avoid burns, do not touch heated items until they have cooled down completely.
• Do not touch the remaining solid directly with your hands (Use a laboratory spoon or a suitable tool).
