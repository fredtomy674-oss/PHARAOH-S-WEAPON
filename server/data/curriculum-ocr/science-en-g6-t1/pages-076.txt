Point! Acidic, Neutral, and Alkaline solutions
Solutions may look similar, but they are differ in their properties Solutions can be divided into 3 groups by how they change the color of litmus paper.
• Acidic solution: Turns blue litmus paper red.
(Example: lemon juice, vinegar, hydrochloric acid)
• Neutral solution: Does not change the color of either litmus paper.
(Example: salt water, sugar water, distilled water)
• Alkaline solution: Turns red litmus paper blue.
(Example: soapy solution, limewater, ammonia solution)

Warm Up Let's group solutions using litmus paper!
Let's use various solutions and observe the color changes.
1 Preparation:
• Solutions (salt water, carbonated water "soda water", dilute hydrochloric acid, baking soda solution, dilute ammonia solution)
• Beaker • Test tube • Test tube rack • Glass rod
• Tweezers • Litmus paper • Filter paper • Safety goggles
2 Experiment:
1 Put blue and red strips of litmus paper on the filter paper.
2 Use a glass rod to add one drop of the solution you want to test onto both litmus strips.
3 Check the color change of the litmus strips and group them into acidic, neutral, or alkaline.
[Caution!]
• When changing the solution, rinse the glass rod with water in a beaker and wipe it off every time.
• Make sure to ventilate the room. Also, wear safety goggles.
