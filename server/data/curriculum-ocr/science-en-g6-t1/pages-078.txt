9-2 What is dissolved in solutions Properties of Solutions Chapter 9

Point! The Identity of Dissolved Substances
! By evaporating a solution, we can find out what was dissolved inside.
! Solids dissolved in water:
When you evaporate the water, the dissolved substances (such as salt or baking soda) will remain at the bottom of the dish.
! Gases dissolved in water:
• Even if you evaporate the water, nothing will remain because the dissolved substance escapes into the air. (Example: sparkling water)
• Sparkling water has carbon dioxide gas dissolved in it.

Warm Up Let's heat and extract what is dissolved!
Let's extract and observe what became invisible by dissolving in water.
1 Preparation:
• Solutions (salt water, carbonated water, dilute hydrochloric acid, baking soda solution, dilute ammonia solution)
• Beaker • Test tube • Test tube rack • Pipette • Evaporating dish
• Portable gas stove • Wire gauze • Safety goggles
2 Experiment:
1 Use a pipette to transfer the solution from the test tube into the evaporating dish.
2 Ask your teacher to heat the solution gently over low heat with a portable gas stove.
3 Turn off the heat while a little liquid is still left, and let the remaining heat dry it completely.
4 Observe what is left.
[Caution!]
• During the experiment, do not bring your face close to the evaporating dish to smell it or breathe it in directly. When smelling, use your hand to waft the smell toward you.
• To avoid burns, do not touch heated objects until they cool down completely.
3 Summary:
• Let's summarize what was left in the evaporating dish in a table.
• Also, write down how the solutions look and smell.
