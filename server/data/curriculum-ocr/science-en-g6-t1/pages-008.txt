1-1 How Things Burn and Air Conditions for Burning Chapter 1

Point! Air Passages and Burning
For things to keep burning, fresh air must constantly flow in and out. In a place where the air is not replaced, things cannot keep burning and can be explained by the experiment of burning a candle inside a glass jar.
Experiment of burning things inside a jar
• When not covered with a lid:
Fresh air is supplied from the surroundings, so the candle keeps burning.
• When covered with a lid:
Once the air inside the jar is used up, the flame will go out.

Warm Up Let's keep things burning in various ways!
Let's do an experiment about how air works when things burn.
1 Preparation:
• Wooden board • Clay • Candle • Candle holder
• Open-ended glass jar ( Bottomless) • Metal lid
• Lighter (used only by the teacher) • Waste tray
2 Experiment:
• Spread the clay flat on the board.
• Stand a candle on the clay, light it, and cover it with the bottomless glass jar.
1 What happens to the flame when you put a lid on the jar?
2 What happens when you cut the clay to create a gap at the bottom?
3 What happens when you take off the lid to make gaps at both the top and bottom?
3 Summary:
• Summarize how the candle burns and how the air moves for each of 1 to 3.
