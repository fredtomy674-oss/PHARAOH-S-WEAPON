Escape Velocity :
It’s fascinating to know that Mercury is the
smallest planet in the solar system and has no
gaseous atmosphere. As a result, an enormous
amount of solar radiation reaches the planet’s
surface, causing its temperature to rise significantly
due to the absorption of that radiation.
When the sun is no longer shining on Mercury-as
the planet rotates-this radiation is emitted back into space, and the planet
cools rapidly because there is no atmosphere to retain the heat.
You might be wondering why Earth retains its atmosphere, while
Mercury does not have one?
Gas molecules move at extremely high
speeds, depending on the mass of the gas
molecule and the temperature. Naturally, the
effective speed of gas molecules, known as
Vrms becomes greater when the molecular
mass is lower and the temperature is higher.
To escape a planet’s gravity, an object-
regardless of its mass-must reach a specific speed called the escape velocity
(Ve), which is a fixed value for any given
planet.
For Earth, the escape velocity is
approximately 11.2 km/s.
The presence of a gas around a planet
is linked to the relationship between the
Vrms of gas molecules and the planet’s
escape velocity (Ve).
If the effective speed of gas molecules (Vrms) is less than Ve, (Vrms< Ve),
the gas molecules cannot escape into space, and the planet retains its
atmosphere.
