Adaptation of Living Organisms to Natural Changes in Temperature:
- Adaptation to Freezing:
The wood frog lives in cold areas in the north such as Alaska and
Canada, where temperatures drop below zero in winter. The frog’s body
partially freezes in winter as its breathing stops and its heart stops beating.
It remains in a state of deep
hibernation.
The frog stores large amounts
of glucose in its vital organs (heart,
liver, brain) before freezing. Glucose
acts as an antifreeze substance
by preventing the formation of ice
crystals in the cells and protecting
them from damage.
When the temperature rises in spring, the ice melts, the heart starts
beating, and vital functions begin to work again.
