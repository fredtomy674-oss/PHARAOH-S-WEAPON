Water Density and Temperature in Polar Regions:
Generally, the liquid volume
increases as its temperature
rises and decreases as its
temperature falls. However,
water is an exception to this
rule.
As the temperature of pure water increases from 0 o C to 4 o C, water
contracts (its volume decreases), and its density increases. The density
of water reaches its maximum value (of 1000 kg/m 3 ) at 4 o C. Above 4 o C,
as temperature increases, water expands (its volume increases), and its
density decreases.
These scientific facts help explain why water in a lake in a polar
region begins to freeze at its surface instead of the bottom. As the air
temperature drops from 4 o C
to 0 o C, the water surface in the
lake expands and becomes less
dense than the water below. This
means that when water freezes
and turns into ice, it becomes
even less dense. This is because
ice molecules are spaced farther
apart than in liquid water. Consequently, ice floats, creating an insulating
layer that prevents the rest of water below from freezing. This allows
aquatic organisms to survive in that environment, which is another reason
why water is considered as an ideal habitat. (Figure 1-16)
