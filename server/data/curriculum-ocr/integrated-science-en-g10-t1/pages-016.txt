Â Hydrolysis of Salts :
Hydrolysis is the reaction occurring when a solid salt dissolves in water,
forming an acid and a base, one or both of which are weak.
Salts are classified into three categories according to their hydrolysis:
(a) Acidic salts :
When an acidic salt dissolves in water, hydrolysis of the salt occurs,
leading to a decrease in concentration of hydroxide ion (OH - ) and an increase
in concentration of hydrogen ion (H + ) , making the salt solution acidic.
Example: Dissolving ammonium chloride salt ( NH 4 Cl ) in water:
(b) Basic salts :
When a basic salt dissolves in water, hydrolysis of the salt occurs, leading
to a decrease in concentration of hydrogen ion (H + ) and an increase in
concentration of hydroxide ion (OH - ), making the salt solution basic.
Example: Dissolving sodium bicarbonate salt ( NaHCO 3 ) in water:

When a neutral salt is dissolved in water, hydrolysis of the salt occurs that
does not affect the equity of the concentration of hydrogen ions (H+) and
hydroxide ions (OH - ), which maintain the salt solution neutral.
