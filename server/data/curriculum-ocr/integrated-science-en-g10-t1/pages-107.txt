Formation of acid rain:
In the atmosphere, a series of reactions lead to the acidity of rain:
First: Formation of sulfuric acid
sulfur dioxide (SO 2 ), emitted from the burning of sulfur-containing fossil
fuels, reacts with oxygen (O 2 ), to form sulfur trioxide (SO 3 ). This reaction
occurs in the presence of catalysts such as sunlight.
2SO 2 (g) + O 2 (g) → 2SO 3 (g)
Then, sulfur trioxide reacts with water droplets in clouds to form sulfuric
acid, which is a very strong acid. (H 2 SO 4 )
SO 3 (g) + H 2 O (l) → H 2 SO 4 (aq)
Second: Formation of nitric acid
Nitrogen oxides (NO x ), which form naturally from lightning or from fuel
combustion emissions at high temperatures, react with water to form nitric
acid (HNO 3 ).
2NO 2 (g) + H 2 O (l) → HNO 2 (aq) + HNO 3 (aq)
In this equation, nitric acid and nitrous acid are formed; generally, the
reaction can be summarized as follows:
4NO 2 (g) + O 2 (g) + 2H 2 O (l) → 4HNO 3 (aq)
