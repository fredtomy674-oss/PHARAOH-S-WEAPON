How do you write a proof in geometry?

When you want to write a geometric proof, you write a series of logical steps go from the givens to the conclusion to prove the validity of what you want to prove.

Example (4)
In the opposite figure:
$\overleftrightarrow{CA} \parallel \overleftrightarrow{BE}$
$m(\angle B) = 55^\circ$, $m(\angle C) = 125^\circ$
Prove that: $\overleftrightarrow{AB} \parallel \overleftrightarrow{CD}$

Given: $\overleftrightarrow{CA} \parallel \overleftrightarrow{BE}$, $\overleftrightarrow{AB}$ is a transversal.
Required to Prove: $\overleftrightarrow{AB} \parallel \overleftrightarrow{CD}$
Proof:
$\because \overleftrightarrow{CA} \parallel \overleftrightarrow{BE}$, and $\overleftrightarrow{AB}$ is a transversal.
$\therefore m(\angle A) = m(\angle B) = 55^\circ$
Alternating interior angles.
$\because m(\angle A) + m(\angle C) = 55^\circ + 125^\circ = 180^\circ$,
and they are interior angles on the same side of the transversal.
$\therefore \overleftrightarrow{AB} \parallel \overleftrightarrow{CD}$

■ Hint
When writing a proof in geometry, it is important to provide logical reasoning for each step.
