■ Using Technology
You can use the calculator
to convert a rational
number in the form of $\frac{a}{b}$ to
a recurring decimal, and
vice versa, as follows:
• To express the fraction $\frac{2}{9}$
in the form of an infinite
(recurring) decimal,
enter $\frac{2}{9}$ on your calculator.
Press the $=$ key,
and then $S \Leftrightarrow D$.
The calculator will display
0.2222222, which is
written as $0.\bar{2}$
• Conversely, to express
the recurring decimal $0.\bar{2}$
in the form of $\frac{a}{b}$,
enter 0.2222222 on your
calculator till you have
full screen of 2s, then
press the $=$ key. The
calculator will display $\frac{2}{9}$

The different Forms of a Rational Number
• Expressing a rational number in the form of
a percentage:
For example: $\frac{1}{2} = 50\%$ , $2\frac{1}{4} = 225\%$
• Expressing a rational number in the form of
a finite decimal:
For example: $\frac{2}{5} = 0.4$ , $1\frac{3}{4} = 1.75$
• Expressing a rational number in the form of an infinite (recurring) decimal:
For example: $\frac{2}{9}$ can be expressed in the form of an infinite decimal,
$0.222...$ or $0.\bar{2}$ (which is read as $0.2$ recurring)

First Addition of Rational Numbers
• If $\frac{a}{c}$ and $\frac{b}{c}$ are two rational numbers with equal denominators, then:
$\frac{a}{c} + \frac{b}{c} = \frac{a + b}{c}$
• If $\frac{a}{b}$ and $\frac{c}{d}$ are two rational numbers with different denominators, then:
$\frac{a}{b} + \frac{c}{d} = \frac{ad + bc}{bd}$

Example 1
Find the result of each of the following in the simplest form:
1 $-\frac{5}{8} + \frac{3}{8}$
2 $\frac{1}{4} + \left(-\frac{1}{3}\right)$
3 $2.6 + 2.\bar{6}$

1 $-\frac{5}{8} + \frac{3}{8} = \frac{-5 + 3}{8} = \frac{-2}{8} = \frac{-1}{4}$
2 $\frac{1}{4} + \left(-\frac{1}{3}\right) = \frac{3 + (-4)}{12} = \frac{-1}{12}$
3 $2.6 + 2.\bar{6} = 2\frac{6}{10} + 2\frac{2}{3} = 2\frac{3}{5} + 2\frac{2}{3} = 2\frac{9}{15} + 2\frac{10}{15}$
$= 4\frac{19}{15} = 5\frac{4}{15}$
