SAS Similarity theorem (Proof is not required)
If an angle of one triangle is congruent to an angle of another triangle and lengths
of the sides including those angles are in proportion, then the triangles are similar

Given: ∠ A ≡ ∠ D, AB/DE = AC/DF
R.t.p.: Δ ABC ~ Δ DEF
Proof: take X ∈ AB where AX = DE
draw XY // BC
and intersect AC at Y
∴ XY // BC ∴ Δ ABC ~ Δ AXY (Corollary) (1)
, AB/AX = AC/AY
∴ AB/DE = AC/EF (given) , AX = DE (construction)
∴ AB/AX = AC/EF , AY = DF
∴ Δ AXY ≡ Δ DEF (SAS congruency theorem)
Δ AXY ~ Δ DEF (2)
from (1), (2) we get: Δ ABC ~ Δ DEF Q.E.D.

Example
7 ABC is a triangle AB = 8cm, AC = 10cm, BC = 12cm, E ∈ AB where AE = 2cm, D ∈ BC
where BD = 4cm.
A Prove that Δ BDE ~ Δ BAC and deduce the length of DE .
B Prove that figure ACDE is a cyclic quadrilateral.

Solution
∴ AB = 8cm, AE = 2cm ∴ BE = 6cm
A In ΔΔ BDE, BAC,:
∠ DBE ≡ ∠ ABC (1)
, BD/BA = 4/8 = 1/2 , BE/BC = 6/12 = 1/2
∴ BD/BA = BE/BC (2)
from (1), (2) ∴ Δ BDE ~ Δ BAC (theorem)
from similarity DE/AC = 1/2
∴ DE = 1/2 AC , DE = 1/2 × 10 = 5cm
