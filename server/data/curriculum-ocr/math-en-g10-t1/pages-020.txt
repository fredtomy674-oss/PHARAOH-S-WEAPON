Forming the quadratic equation whose two roots are known
Let L and M be the roots of the quadratic equation: ax² + bx + c = 0, a ≠ 0
Dividing both sides of the equation by a: ∴ x² + b/a x + c/a = 0
i.e. x² - (-b/a) x + c/a = 0
∴ L and M are the two roots of the quadratic equation , L + M = - b/a , LM = c/a
∴ The quadratic equation whose roots are L and M is: x² - (L + M) x + L M = 0

Example
4 Form the quadratic equation whose two roots are 4 and -3 .

Solution
Let the two roots of the equation be l and M
∴ L + M = 4 + (-3) = 1, L M = 4 (- 3) = - 12,
∴ The quadratic equation formula is: x² - (L + M) x + L M = 0
∴ The equation is: x² - x - 12 = 0

5 Form the quadratic equation whose two roots are: -2+2i / 1+i and -2-4i / 2-i

Solution
Let the two roots be L and M
L = -2 + 2i / 1 + i × 1 - i / 1 - i = 4i / 2 = 2i
M = -2 - 4i / 2 - i × 2 + i / 2 + i = - 10i / 5 = - 2 i
L + M = 2 i - 2i = 0
, L M = 2 i × - 2i = - 4i² = 4
∴ The quadratic equation whose two roots are L and M is: x² - (L + M) x + L M = 0
∴ x² + 4 = 0
