$$\frac{(2+\mathrm{i})(2-\mathrm{i})}{3+4\mathrm{i}}=x+\mathrm{i}y$$

Solution
$$\frac{4-\mathrm{i}^{2}}{3+4\mathrm{i}}=x+\mathrm{i}y$$
$$\frac{4+1}{3+4\mathrm{i}}\times\frac{3-4\mathrm{i}}{3-4\mathrm{i}}=x+\mathrm{i}y$$
$$\frac{5(3-4\mathrm{i})}{25}=x+\mathrm{i}y$$
$$\frac{3}{5}-\frac{4}{5}\mathrm{i}=x+\mathrm{i}y$$
$$\text{i.e.: } x=\frac{3}{5}\text{ , } y=-\frac{4}{5}$$

6 Electricity: Find the total electric current intensity passing through two resistances connected on parallel in a closed circuit, if the current intensity in the first resistance is $5-3\mathrm{i}$ ampere and the second resistance is $2+\mathrm{i}$ ampere ( given that the total current intensity equals the sum of the two current intensities which passes the two resistances)

Solution
$\because$ The total electric current intensity = the sum of the two current intensities passing in the two resistances.
$\therefore = (5 - 3\mathrm{i}) + (2 + \mathrm{i})$
$\therefore = (5 + 2) + (-3 + 1)\mathrm{i}$
$\therefore = 7 - 2\mathrm{i}\text{ ampere}$
