The unit circle
In any orthogonal coordinate system, a circle of centre at the
origin point and of radius equals the unit of length is called
a unit circle.
★ The unit circle intersects the x -axis at the two points
A (1, 0) and B (-1, 0), and intersects the y-axis at the two
points C (0, 1) and D (0, -1).
★ If (x, y) are the coordinates of any point on the unit circle:
then x ∈ [-1, 1] , y ∈ [-1, 1].
where x² + y² = 1
Phythagorean theorem

The basic trigonometric functions of an angle
for any directed angle in the standard position, and its terminal side intersects the unit circle
at the point B(x, y) and its measure θ, it is possible to define the following functions:
1- cosine of the angle θ = x-coordinate of point B
i.e.: cos θ = x
2- sine of the angle θ = y- coordinate of point B
i.e.: sin θ = y
3- tangent of the angle θ = y-coordinate of point B / x-coordinate of point B
i.e.: tan θ = y/x where x ≠ 0 , tan θ = sin θ / cos θ where cos θ ≠ 0

Notice that: the ordered pair (x, y) of any point on the unit circle is written in the form
(cos θ, sin θ)
If the point C (3/5, 4/5) is the point of intersection of the terminal side of a directed angle of
measure θ with the unit circle
then: cos θ = 3/5 , sin θ = 4/5 and tan θ = 4/3

Reciprocals of the basic trigonometric functions
For any directed angle in the standard position and its terminal side intersects the unit circle
at the point B(x, y) and its measure is θ, then there are the following functions :
1- secant of the angle θ : sec θ = 1/x = 1/cos θ where x ≠ 0
2- Cosecant of the angle θ : csc θ = 1/y = 1/sin θ where y ≠ 0
3- Cotangent of the angle θ : cot θ = x/y = 1/tan θ where y ≠ 0
