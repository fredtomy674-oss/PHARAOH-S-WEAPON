Key - Terms
► Root
► Discriminant
Learn
Discriminant
The two roots of the quadratic equation $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ where $a \neq 0$ and
$a, b, c \in \mathbb{R}$
are: $\displaystyle \frac{-b + \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$ , $\displaystyle \frac{-b - \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$
and the two roots contain the expression $\sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}$
the expression $b^2 - 4ac$ is called the discriminant of the quadratic equation
and is used to determine the type of the two roots of the equation.
Example
Materials
► Scientific Calculator
1 Determine the type of the two roots of each of the following
equations:
A $5x^2 + x - 7 = 0$
C $-x^2 + 5x - 30 = 0$
B $x^2 - 2x + 1 = 0$
Solution
To determine the type of the two roots:
A $a = 5 , b = 1 , c = -7$
the discriminant = $b^2 - 4ac$
$= 1 - 4 \times 5 (-7) = 141$
$\because$ the discriminant is positive, $\therefore$ there are two real different roots.
B $a = 1 , b = -2 , c = 1$
the discriminant = $b^2 - 4ac$
$= 4 - 4 \times 1 \times 1 = 0$
$\because$ the discriminant equals zero, $\therefore$ the the two roots are real and equal.
