Finding the length of the interior and the exterior bisectors of an angle of a triangle

well known problem
If AD bisects ∠ A in Δ ABC internally and intersects BC at D
then: AD = √ AB × AC - BD × DC (Proof is not required)

Given: ABC is a triangle, AD bisects ∠ BAC internally, AD ∩ BC = {D}
R.t.p.: (AD)² = AB × AC - BD × DC
Proof: Draw a circle passes through Δ ABC
and intersects AD at E, draw BE
then: Δ ACD ~ Δ AEB (why)?, AD / AB = AC / AE
∴ AD × AE = AB × AC
AD × (AD + DE) = AB × AC
(AD)² = AB × AC - AD × DE
(AD)² = AB × AC - BD × DC
i.e.: AD = √ AB × AC - BD × DC

Remember
AD × DE = BD × DC

Example
4 ABC is a triangle in which AB = 27cm, AC = 15cm. AD bisects ∠ A and intersects BC at D.
If BD = 18cm. Calculate the length of AD.

Solution
∴ AD bisects ∠ BAC  ∴ BD / DC = AB / AC
then 18 / DC = 27 / 15  ∴ D C = 10cm
∴ AD = √ AB × AC - BD × DC
∴ AD = √ 27 × 15 - 18 × 10 = √ 225 = 15 cm
